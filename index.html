Research · IIT Bombay · 2025

The fitness landscape of p53 mutations

My BTech thesis, carried out at IIT Bombay under Prof. Supreet Saini. I analysed how ~10,000 mutations in TP53, the most frequently mutated gene in human cancer, change cancer-cell fitness.

  • Gaussian mixture models show a two-peaked fitness landscape; KS, Mann–Whitney U and Cohen's d compare mutation classes.
  • Some "silent" synonymous mutations still change fitness; a sliding-window codon adaptation index maps fast and slow translating regions.
  • Exhaustive epistasis scan of 797 mutation pairs: 535 negative, 160 none, 52 sign and 50 positive.
  • Reproducible: GitHub Actions re-runs every notebook on each push, and each one opens in Colab.
  • Python
  • pandas
  • NumPy
  • SciPy
  • scikit-learn
  • GitHub Actions

04About

A scientist's habits, an engineer's job.

I grew up in a family of doctors and studied bioinformatics and data science, which meant learning to distrust a result until the statistics backed it. My thesis at IIT Bombay was months of exactly that: thousands of mutations, careful tests, and figures that had to survive questions.

Since then I have mostly been building: chatbots and agents that answer from a company's own data, pipelines that clean and rank messy web data, and small products people can try. I like work where the AI has to be useful and checkable at the same time.

I am now doing an MSc in Artificial Intelligence at the National College of Ireland, including a module on data governance, ethics and sustainability. Outside code, you will find me at the gym six days a week.
